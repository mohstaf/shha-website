# SHHA Website – Structure, Content and Launch Plan

Nov 23, 2026 · @ALI

## Purpose and audiences

The website has two jobs: show funders and partners that SHHA is a credible, well-governed national organisation, and tell Somali families where to get help. Every page serves one of these five audiences.

| Audience | What they need from the site | Language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patients and families | Warning signs, where to get tested, how to register, a phone or WhatsApp contact | Somali first |
| Health workers | Clinical resources, training opportunities, referral contacts | English and Somali |
| Funders and international partners (WFH, TIF, NNHF, Africa CDC, universities) | Governance, programmes, evidence of need, reports, contact person | English |
| Government (Ministry of Health, Ministry of Interior) | National burden, SHHA's role, policy asks | Somali and English |
| Somali diaspora and donors | How to donate, volunteer or connect SHHA with hospitals abroad | English, Somali; Arabic later |

Principles:

* Bilingual from launch: every page in Somali and English.
* Mobile first: most visitors will use phones on slow connections, so keep pages light and images small.
* Only verified facts: every figure carries a source and date; partners are named only with their agreement.
* Privacy: no patient names, photos or stories without written consent; the registry form collects the minimum needed.
* Kept current: at least one news post a month. An outdated site is worse than no site for funders.

## About pages

Funders read the About section first, so it must show governance, not only good intentions. Copy is taken from the SHHA profile.

**Who we are**

The Somali Haemophilia and Haemoglobinopathies Association (SHHA) is a national, non-profit, volunteer-led organisation based in Mogadishu. It is dedicated to improving the lives of people living with haemophilia and other inherited bleeding disorders, sickle cell disease and thalassaemia across Somalia. SHHA brings together patients, families, clinicians and laboratory professionals with one shared purpose: to make sure no Somali child or adult with an inherited blood disorder goes undiagnosed, untreated or unsupported.

**Vision:** A Somalia where every person with an inherited blood disorder is diagnosed early, treated safely and empowered to live a full and healthy life.

**Mission:** To improve diagnosis, treatment and quality of life for people with inherited blood disorders through patient advocacy, awareness, education, partnership and support for the strengthening of the national health system.

**Core values**

* Patient-centred: the needs and voices of patients and families guide everything we do.
* Equity: care for everyone, in every region, regardless of gender, age, clan or income.
* Integrity and transparency: honest use of resources and open reporting to members and partners.
* Partnership: working with government, clinicians and national and international partners.
* Evidence: decisions guided by data, international guidelines and patient experience.

**Our objectives**

1. Identify and register people with inherited blood disorders across Somalia.
2. Advocate with government and partners for access to diagnosis, treatment and comprehensive care.
3. Raise public and professional awareness of haemophilia, bleeding disorders and haemoglobinopathies.
4. Educate patients, families and caregivers, and build the capacity of health workers.
5. Facilitate access to essential treatment, including donated clotting factor concentrates.
6. Promote research and data that guide policy and services.
7. Mobilise resources to sustain the Association's work.

**Our story**

SHHA was founded in Mogadishu in 2026 by patients, families and health professionals who saw children with bleeding disorders and haemoglobinopathies going undiagnosed and untreated. What began as a small group following a preliminary register of patients has grown into a national association with a clear mandate for both bleeding disorders and haemoglobinopathies.

| Year | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 | Engagement began with the World Federation of Hemophilia and regional and international partners |
| 2026 | Registration initiated with the Ministry of Interior, Federal Affairs and Reconciliation |
| 2026 | Mandate broadened to haemoglobinopathies, as SHHA |
| 2026 | Association founded in Mogadishu; preliminary national patient register established |

**Board and governance**

SHHA is governed by an elected Board and a General Assembly of members, and is advised by a Medical Advisory Committee of haematologists, paediatricians and laboratory professionals. The Association is run by volunteers and works closely with clinicians across Somalia.

## Blood disorder pages

Each condition page uses the same six headings, written in plain language for families and checked by the Medical Advisory Committee before publishing. Keep each page to about 400 words.

| Heading | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| What is it? | One paragraph, no jargon |
| How is it inherited? | Simple diagram; who can carry it |
| Warning signs | Bulleted list a parent can recognise |
| Getting tested | Which test, where in Somalia, what it costs if known |
| Treatment and living well | What care exists, what families can do at home |
| Get help | WhatsApp, Register with SHHA, link to treatment centres |

**Haemophilia and other bleeding disorders**

Haemophilia is an inherited condition in which the blood does not clot properly because a clotting factor (factor VIII or IX) is missing or low. It mainly affects boys and men; women can carry the gene and some have bleeding symptoms too. In Somalia, many boys are first noticed because of heavy bleeding after circumcision. Warning signs: bleeding that will not stop after circumcision, a cut, a tooth extraction or an injection; large bruises from small bumps; swollen, painful joints or muscles. Related pages: von Willebrand disease and rare factor deficiencies.

**Sickle cell disease**

Sickle cell disease is an inherited condition in which red blood cells become stiff and sickle-shaped. They block small blood vessels and break down early, causing pain, anaemia and damage to organs. A child inherits it when both parents carry the sickle gene. Warning signs: painful swelling of hands and feet in babies; sudden severe pain in bones, chest or belly; frequent infections and fever; pale skin or yellow eyes.

**Thalassaemia**

Thalassaemia is an inherited condition in which the body makes too little normal haemoglobin, the part of red blood cells that carries oxygen. Mild forms may cause no symptoms; severe forms appear in early childhood and need regular blood transfusions. Warning signs: pale skin, tiredness, poor growth and feeding, a swollen belly, changes in face bones.

**Inheritance and carrier testing**

All of these conditions are inherited. Marriage between close relatives raises the chance that both parents carry the same gene. A simple blood test can show whether someone is a carrier. This page explains carrier testing before marriage and during pregnancy, without blame and in a culturally respectful way.

Each page ends with: This information does not replace advice from a qualified health worker.

## Other pages

**Get help**

* Treatment and testing centres: a table of hospital, region, tests available (for example CBC, coagulation screen, factor assay, haemoglobin electrophoresis), contact. List only centres you have confirmed.
* Register with SHHA: a short form (name, age, sex, region, diagnosis if known, phone, consent tick box). Explain why: the register helps SHHA plan services and request donated treatment. Data is confidential and never published.
* Living with a blood disorder: practical advice for families on school, injuries, pain, nutrition, travel, and emergencies.
* For health workers: links to free WFH and TIF guidelines and e-courses, the ISTH bleeding assessment tool, and how to refer a patient to SHHA.

## Technical setup and policies

Use WordPress with a free bilingual plugin, so one trained volunteer can update the site from a phone without a developer.

| Item | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Domain | haemophilia-somalia.org (already owned); renew for several years |
| Platform | WordPress on managed hosting with SSL (https), daily backups |
| Languages | English and Somali at launch (Polylang or WPML); Arabic later |
| Theme | Lightweight, mobile-first theme; colours from the logo (navy, Somali blue, red) |
| Forms | Contact, registration and membership forms with consent tick boxes; results to one SHHA inbox |
| Email | Official addresses on the domain: info@, president@, registry@ |
| Donations | Bank transfer and mobile money first; online card payments only once the bank account is in SHHA's name |
| Analytics | Simple visitor statistics to report reach to funders |
| Accessibility | Readable fonts (16 px minimum), alt text on every image, good colour contrast |
| Social media | Facebook, LinkedIn, X and WhatsApp channel linked in the footer |

Policies to publish (one page each, in the footer):

* Privacy policy: what the forms collect, why, who sees it, how to ask for deletion.
* Medical disclaimer: the site gives information, not medical advice; in an emergency go to the nearest hospital.
* Consent policy for photos and stories: written consent from the patient or parent before any photo or story is used.
* Conflict of interest and funding statement: who funds SHHA and how industry support is managed.

## Launch plan

A Phase 1 site can be live in about 8 weeks with one coordinator and a part-time web volunteer or freelancer. Week 1 is the week the work starts.

**Weeks 1–2: decisions and content gathering**

* ☐ Choose one official email address and update the poster
* ☐ Appoint a website coordinator (content owner) and a web volunteer or freelancer
* ☐ Confirm board list, short bios and photos
* ☐ Confirm treatment and testing centres to list
* ☐ Collect 10–15 real photos with written consent
* ☐ Ask a designer for a simplified logo for small sizes

**Weeks 3–4: writing and translation**

* ☐ Finalise English copy from this document
* ☐ Translate all Phase 1 pages into Somali
* ☐ Medical Advisory Committee reviews the condition pages
* ☐ Write 3–5 news posts

**Weeks 5–7: build**

* ☐ Set up hosting, WordPress, SSL and the bilingual plugin
* ☐ Build pages to the mockup layout; add forms and footer policies
* ☐ Set up official email addresses on the domain
* ☐ Test on phones and slow connections

**Week 8: launch**

* ☐ Final check of every link, number and form
* ☐ Launch and announce on WhatsApp, Facebook and LinkedIn; email partners (WFH, TIF, NNHF, regional societies)
* ☐ Add the website link to email signatures, the SHHA profile and grant applications
