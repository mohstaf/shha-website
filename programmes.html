Patient registry

Building a confidential national register of people with inherited blood disorders.

The register helps us plan services and request donated treatment. It collects only what is needed, and personal data is never published.

SHHA is starting the first national register now. Register with SHHA

Awareness and education

Family education days, materials in Somali, and national awareness days.

We help families and health workers recognise the warning signs early. Each year we mark World Haemophilia Day (17 April), International Thalassaemia Day (8 May) and World Sickle Cell Day (19 June).

What we are doing now: preparing for World Haemophilia Day 2027.

Capacity building

Training doctors, nurses and laboratory staff in diagnosis and comprehensive care.

Without trained staff and capable laboratories, patients go undiagnosed. Free guidelines and courses are on our For health workers page.

Access to treatment

Seeking donated clotting factor concentrates and essential treatment, used safely under qualified clinicians.

There is no clotting factor concentrate in the Somali public health system and no haemophilia treatment centre. Any donated treatment will be given only by qualified clinicians.

Haemoglobinopathies

Generating evidence on sickle cell disease and thalassaemia and developing care pathways.

Somalia has no newborn screening and no national data for either condition. We broadened our mandate to include them in 2026.

Advocacy

Working with national authorities to make inherited blood disorders a health priority.

We want diagnosis and care to become part of the national health system. Our case draws on register data, international guidelines and patient experience.

Somali Haemophilia and Haemoglobinopathies Association

A national, non-profit, volunteer-led organisation based in Mogadishu, for people living with inherited blood disorders.

Registration with the Ministry of Interior, Federal Affairs and Reconciliation is in progress.
