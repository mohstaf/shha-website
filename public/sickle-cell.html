Sickle cell disease

An inherited condition of the red blood cells. With early diagnosis and good care, children can live full lives.

On this page

  2. How is it inherited?
  3. Warning signs
  4. Getting tested
  5. Treatment and living well

What is it?

Sickle cell disease is an inherited condition in which red blood cells become stiff and sickle-shaped. They block small blood vessels and break down early, causing pain, anaemia and damage to organs.

It also makes some infections harder to fight.

How is it inherited?

A child inherits it when both parents carry the sickle gene. A carrier has sickle cell trait, is usually healthy, and may not know it.

A simple blood test shows whether you are a carrier. Read about carrier testing.

Warning signs

  • Painful swelling of hands and feet in babies
  • Sudden severe pain in bones, chest or belly
  • Frequent infections and fever
  • Pale skin or yellow eyes

In an emergency, such as heavy bleeding or a severe pain crisis, go to the nearest hospital immediately.

Getting tested

A health worker will usually ask for a complete blood count (CBC) and a sickle solubility screen. The screen cannot tell the trait from the disease, so haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC is used to confirm.

Newborn screening is not yet available in Somalia. If sickle cell runs in your family, ask about testing your child early. See the testing centres.

Treatment and living well

  • Drink plenty of fluids and keep warm.
  • Treat pain early with pain relief a health worker advises.
  • Prevent infection: keep vaccines up to date, and give penicillin and folic acid if a doctor prescribes them.
  • Prevent malaria with a treated bed net.

Some people benefit from hydroxyurea, a medicine that can reduce pain crises. It must be started and checked by a specialist.

Get care urgently for fever, chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness, or severe pain that does not ease at home.

Get help

Worried about yourself or your child? Contact SHHA. We will tell you where to get tested and treated, and register you so you are not forgotten.
