Inheritance and carrier testing

All of these conditions are inherited. A simple blood test can show whether someone is a carrier.

How genes are passed on

We have two copies of most genes, one from each parent. A carrier has one changed copy and is usually healthy.

Marriage between close relatives raises the chance that both parents carry the same gene.

Being a carrier is no one's fault. Nobody chooses their genes. This page explains carrier testing without blame and in a culturally respectful way.

Recessive conditions

Sickle cell disease, thalassaemia, severe von Willebrand disease and most rare factor deficiencies happen when a child inherits a changed gene from both parents.

{{> diagram-recessive}}

If only one parent is a carrier, children will not have the condition, but each has a 1 in 2 chance of being a carrier.

Haemophilia

The haemophilia gene is on the X chromosome. A mother who carries it can pass it to her children, and sons who inherit it have haemophilia.

{{> diagram-xlinked}}

Sometimes the gene changes for the first time, so there is no family history. Read about haemophilia.

Carrier testing

For sickle cell and thalassaemia, the test is a complete blood count (CBC) and haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC. For haemophilia, a factor level and, where available, a genetic test.

A normal factor level does not always rule out being a haemophilia carrier. Results are private and shared only with you.

Before marriage

Testing before marriage gives a couple time to understand their results. If both are carriers, a health worker can explain the chances and the options.

The decision belongs to the couple and their families. Some choose to talk with elders or religious scholars too.

During pregnancy

Ask for a carrier test early in pregnancy. If the mother is a carrier, the father can be tested too.

A woman who may carry haemophilia should tell her midwife or doctor, so the birth can be planned safely. A baby boy should be tested before circumcision.

Relatives

When one person is diagnosed, parents, brothers, sisters and cousins may want testing too. For haemophilia, this includes sisters and aunts on the mother's side.

Offer testing gently. Each person decides for themselves.

Get help

SHHA can tell you where to get a carrier test.
