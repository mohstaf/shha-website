How SHHA can help

Three simple steps, from the first message to the national register.

  1. Message usOn WhatsApp, at any time of day or night, or call us.
  2. Get testedWe tell you which hospital or laboratory near you can do the right tests.
  3. Join the registerWith your agreement, we add you to the confidential national register, so you are not forgotten.

Go to hospital straight away

Do not wait to contact SHHA first. Tell the staff the diagnosis as soon as you arrive.

  • Bleeding that does not stop, or any head injury in a bleeding disorder
  • A pain crisis that does not ease at home
  • Fever in a child with sickle cell disease
  • Chest pain, difficulty breathing, sudden weakness or a fit

What the tests mean

CBC
Complete blood count. Shows anaemia and is often the first test.
Coagulation screen
Shows whether the blood takes longer than usual to clot.
Factor assay
Measures one clotting factor, such as factor VIII or IX, and confirms the type of haemophilia.
Haemoglobin electrophoresis
Finds sickle cell disease, thalassaemia and carriers.

Living with a blood disorder

General advice for families. If your health worker advises something different, follow them.

School

Give the teacher a note with the diagnosis, what to do in an emergency and your phone number. Ask for water, toilet and rest breaks.

Injuries and bleeding

Press a cut firmly with a clean cloth for 10 minutes. Rest a swollen joint, raise it and seek care. Never give aspirin or ibuprofen.

Pain

In a sickle cell pain crisis, give plenty to drink, keep the person warm and let them rest. Do not use ice on the painful area.

Nutrition and fluids

Give a varied diet. With sickle cell, drink water often, more in hot weather. With thalassaemia, avoid iron unless a doctor advises it.

Travel

Carry a letter with the diagnosis, your doctor’s number and enough medicine. With sickle cell disease, ask a doctor before flying.

Before any procedure

Tell every health worker about the condition before an injection, tooth extraction or operation. Avoid injections into the muscle.
